# Changelog

Notable changes to All Hands Care (formerly AfterVisit). The newest entries are at the top.

## Unreleased

- Optional sign-in for people in a circle. From their link, a person can tap "Save this to my account" (a card on Today) and sign in with Google; one more tap, "Save to <their email>", ties their link to that account. Nobody has to: links work as before without it. After that, the link opens only when they are signed in with that account, so a forwarded copy, or a phone that opened the link before, asks for that sign-in instead; someone signed in with another account is told so, with "Use another Google account". Their role and what they see still come from the caregiver's choices. `/circles` lists every circle they run and every circle they help with (the person's name and their role), and a switcher at the top of circle screens moves between them; someone who only helps is not shown the "Start a care circle" tile, but can still start their own from a link under the list. On People, the caregiver sees "Signed in" and the person's Google email, and "Undo" makes the link open on its own again. Removing someone and "New link" work as before. Migration 0025: only the server can tie a link to an account, never to the circle's own caregiver, one person per account in each circle, and deleting a Google account clears its ties. The home page, FAQ, About, For care teams, the guide, the privacy policy and the terms say so; the privacy policy and terms carry the date, and the consent version for new circles is 2026-11-17.

- Database: `circle_owner_id` now answers only the circle's own caregiver (migration 0024). Before, any signed-in user could pass any circle id and get that circle's owner user id back; Supabase's Security Advisor flagged it. Everyone else now gets nothing. Its one use, the check that a caregiver's edit keeps the circle's owner, works as before.

- Larger text for older caregivers. The app now follows the text size set on the person's phone or computer (16 px by default, a little smaller on wide screens), and its working sizes grow with it: names 18 px, buttons, labels and medicine names 16 px, notes 15 px, and nothing someone must read below 14 px (13 px only for short tags like "New"). A medicine's schedule and stop date, a reminder's owner and due date, and the dates under Recent changes moved up to 14 px. Large text on the handoff page is 120% of that. Text boxes on phones stay 16 px, so the iPhone does not zoom. "Good to know" on Review uses the app's own violet colours; the dimmed Continue is easier to read; Print shows on phones again, under the medicines list on the Medications page. Update the circle shows the visit's date and clinician at the top with "Not right? Change it", since dates like "in 2 weeks" are counted from that day and The visit has no review button of its own.

- Review has an "I reviewed it" button on Medicines, Next steps and Watch for (the last two when they have something), at the foot of each, on the left. Continue, on the right of the bar, opens once those are reviewed; before that the bar says which are left. Items marked "Please check this" still hold their section's button until they are looked at, and "Show me" goes to them. Each part has its own colour: Medicines green, The visit blue, Next steps amber, Watch for orange, Good to know violet, Questions grey.

- Reminders: a labelled "Mark done" button instead of a bare circle. A finished reminder stays where it was, crossed out, until the page is left, instead of vanishing. Tapping "Done" again asks "Not done yet?" before opening it again. On a reminder nobody has taken, the caregiver also has "Give to…", which lists the people in the circle to hand it to in one tap, next to "I'll do it".

- Medications: "Taking now" is a bold green heading. A stopped medicine has its name, strength, schedule and instructions crossed out in red, with the stop date in red. "Recent changes" is folded away and opens with a tap.

- "Tell Anna" in "Let people know what changed" no longer replaces an older link by opening it. Someone with an older link gets the note that a new one stops the old one working and a "Make a new link" button; after that tap, the message says it is a new link. The box's text is one short line.

- Phones read less zoomed in. The text size is 14 px on phones as on computers (it was 15 px on phones). Text boxes on phones are 16 px, so tapping one no longer makes the iPhone zoom the whole page. Page titles are smaller on phones; Privacy moves from the top bar to the note at the foot of the page so the bar stays on one line; Print is hidden on phones. The Calendar's week view is a plain list by day on phones instead of a grid to slide sideways, and its subtitle is the person's name only. Large text on the handoff page stays 120%.

- A PDF can be picked from a phone. On the scan page, "Choose photos" is now "Choose photos / files" and takes photos or a PDF; before, it took photos only, so the iPhone's Files list greyed out PDFs (a 1.4 MB, several-page after-visit summary could not be tapped). The separate "Use a PDF instead" link is gone. A PDF larger than 4 MB is turned away with a plain message, on the phone and by the server alike (Vercel cannot take a larger one in one piece); photos of the pages still work for those.

- "Add to Home Screen" is a small button in the top bar of every circle screen and the scan pages, instead of a card further down the page that stayed hidden for good once closed. On Android, and Chrome or Edge on a computer, one tap shows the browser's own install prompt. On iPhone and iPad, which have no such prompt for websites, the tap opens a short sheet with the three steps and a picture of where Share is, ending with "I added it". The button is gone when the app is opened from the Home Screen, after the browser's prompt is accepted, or after "I added it" (an iPhone never tells a website it was added, so the button stays in Safari until then). The home page card stays.

- Review is shorter and has one button. The check mark on each section is gone: the caregiver compares the draft with the original and taps "Looks right" at the bottom. While a medicine or next step marked "Please check this" (or a medicine with no name) is waiting, that button says how many and "Show me" takes the caregiver to it; the update page checks again, as before. Each section is a compact card; the visit shows its date, clinician and summary, with a pencil to change them; Watch for, Good to know and Questions show only when they have something, with a small "Missed something?" button to add one. "Who will do this" and "By when" are no longer on Review for a visit going into a circle: they are picked on Update, next to each appointment and reminder. A follow-up appointment the summary gives no day for is listed with the other appointments on Update; with a day picked it goes on the calendar, and without one it goes in as a reminder. A visit shared on its own (not into a circle) keeps them on Review.

- Update the circle has fewer words and steps. The progress strip is Upload, Review, Update (the done screen is the end of Update). Each medicine, appointment and reminder row can be tapped anywhere to tick or untick it; each reminder has a "By" date beside "Who does it?"; the list of visit notes is one line saying they are kept; and the Update button stays at the bottom of a phone screen.

- The done screen shows everyone's link. "Send each person their link" lists each person put down for something (or added on Update), then anyone else in the circle who can see what changed and has not seen it yet. Each row shows the person's own link and has Text (Messages, with their number when the circle has one, for everyone on the list, and a message), Email (the mail app, with their address when the circle has one, and the same message) and Copy (the link alone). Since opening Messages or the mail app, or copying, is not sending, the row then asks "Sent it to Anna?": only "Yes, sent" marks the person as told; "Not yet" (or no answer) keeps them on the list of people who have not heard. Links load by themselves; an older link is still only replaced on a tap of "Make a new link". This replaces the separate "Text each person what they are down for" and "Let people know what changed" boxes on this screen.

- "Let people know what changed" on the circle's pages asks the same "Sent it to Anna?" after Email, Text message or Copy link; only "Yes, sent" marks the person as told, and Done without it keeps them on the list. Before, any tap, or Done, counted as told. Text message there, and Share on the People page, now fill in the person's number when the circle has one.

- Signing in from "Change allergies" on the Emergency page now comes back to just the allergies, with Back to Emergency; the time zone in Settings sits in its section without a second box and is shown, not changeable, to anyone but the caregiver; the Emergency and time zone buttons use the shared text size and button styles; and the end-to-end tests, iPhone Safari included, run again on every pull request.

- Update the circle (the last step of a visit) is simpler about who helps. Each appointment and reminder offers Me, each person in the circle, and "Add someone"; nobody is picked to start with, and tapping a picked name clears it. "Decide later", "Needs someone" and "Anyone can offer" are gone from this page (the Calendar and Reminders pages keep them). "Add someone" opens a short form: a name, who they are (Family, Care manager, Home aide, Day program) and a phone number to text their link to. They join the circle when the circle is updated, only if still picked for something, and see the calendar and reminders (People can change that). Afterwards, the done screen lists each person put down for something with "Text" (the Messages app opens with their number and a message) and "Copy". The message has their own link and says when (an appointment's day and time, how many reminders) but not what the appointment is for, since a text is not private. People texted here are left out of "Let people know what changed".

- Emergency contacts are added right on the Emergency page: "Add an emergency contact" opens a short form (name, who they are, phone) and saves without leaving the page, someone in the circle who has a number can be added in one tap, and each contact has a Remove button. Only the caregiver sees these; people with a link see the contacts and their call buttons as before.

- Review has a check mark beside each section again, instead of two buttons in the bar at the bottom: Medicines, The visit, and each of Next steps, Watch for, Good to know and Questions that has something in it. Tap "Mark checked" once a section matches the original (tap again to undo); the bar counts them and Continue opens once all are ticked. The rules stay: Medicines cannot be ticked while a medicine marked "Please check this" or with no name is waiting, Next steps likewise, and the update page checks again. Medicines always need a tick, even when none were read, with a note to add any the summary names.

- Medicines listed only under "Pick up these medications at <pharmacy>" (Epic's after-visit summaries) are now read as new medicines, with the dose left blank when the page gives none, by both OpenAI and the built-in reader; before, OpenAI made them a pick-up task and the medicines list came back empty. Vaccines or shots given in the clinic are left out of the medicines. The accuracy set has a new fictional case for this. Review now says, in small type under its title, which reader read the summary (AI from OpenAI, or All Hands Care's built-in reader on the server or on the phone) and to check each item against the original. When OpenAI fails on pasted text and the built-in reader takes over, the answer now says so instead of naming OpenAI.

- Calendar link times are right in Google Calendar, Apple Calendar and Outlook. A medicine at 7:00 showed at 2:00 in Google Calendar: the file gave times with no time zone, and Google reads those as UTC. Each circle now keeps the time zone where the person lives (from the caregiver's phone or computer when the circle is started, or the first time an existing circle is opened), and every timed entry in the link carries it, with daylight saving handled. The Add to your calendar page and Settings say which time zone is used, with a list to pick the one where the person lives (Eastern, Central, Mountain, Arizona, Pacific, Alaska, Hawaii, or this device's), for a caregiver who lives somewhere else. "Today" on Today and the Calendar page, and the date a medicine is stopped (by hand or from a visit), are now the date where the person lives, not the server's UTC date, so an evening change is no longer dated tomorrow. The privacy page lists the time zone among what is kept.

- On the Emergency page, "Add an emergency contact" (or "Change emergency contacts") and a new "Add allergies" (or "Change allergies") are clear buttons for the caregiver. Each opens only that part of the care summary, with a back button to Emergency, and saving returns to Emergency with the change shown. Before, the link was faint and opened the whole care summary editor, which then went to the Care summary page.

- The site no longer calls All Hands Care a nonprofit (home page, link previews and the privacy page). It is free and open source; it is not a registered nonprofit.

- Next.js 16 (16.4.6), with the newer React it brings. `middleware.ts` is now `proxy.ts`, as Next.js 16 names it; it does the same work. Builds use Turbopack. ESLint uses eslint-config-next's own flat config; its new React Compiler rules are off for now.

- Delete a medication added by mistake. A medication's own page (Edit on the list) now ends with "Added by mistake?" and a Delete button, which asks once on the page before anything happens and points to Stop for a medicine that was really taken. Deleting removes it and its history; Stop still keeps the record with the date.

- Buttons no longer stay on "Saving" or "Adding" after a save. Saving a phone number (People, Settings, the Emergency page) or stopping or restarting a medicine could leave the button stuck and pressed down until the page was reloaded, and "Add" on Reminders sometimes stayed on "Adding"; what was saved was kept. These buttons now come back as soon as the save returns. Forms that save and then move to another page, and "I'll go" and "I'll do it", no longer ask for a second refresh on top of the one the save already does. Underneath, the page sometimes never redrew after a save at all (the medicine stayed under "Taking now", a new reminder did not appear) although the save went through; this came from how the version of React inside Next.js 15.5 reads a streamed answer, and is fixed by the move to Next.js 16 below.

- Terms of use and a security policy. A new Terms page, linked from every page's footer and from the sign-in page, says in plain words that All Hands Care is not for emergencies and does not give medical advice, that what it reads from a visit summary must be checked against the paper, who may use it (18 and older, with permission to add someone's care information), how to keep links safe, what is not allowed, that a caregiver's information stays theirs, how a circle or the service ends, its limits, and how to reach us (admin@allhandscare.org, or privacy@allhandscare.org for privacy). The "terms" tick when starting a circle now reads "I agree to the terms of use. All Hands Care does not give medical advice and is not for emergencies.", with links to the terms and the privacy policy, and new circles record the new consent version. `SECURITY.md` says how to report a security problem, how to test safely, and the steps taken if information is exposed.

- Phone numbers and an Emergency page. The caregiver can add a phone number for each person on People (when adding them or by tapping their name), and their own in Settings or on People; a number is never required. A person can add or change their own from their link. The new Emergency page, in the menu for the caregiver and on every link, lists the care summary's emergency contacts, then the people in the circle who have a number, then allergies, each number a large button that calls it; it prints cleanly to keep by the phone. A person's number is seen by the caregiver and by family and care managers; the caregiver's by everyone in the circle. The privacy page says so, with the date of the change. Also: pressing Done on the scan page before its circle has been looked up no longer turns a circle visit into a stand-alone handoff; Done waits, and if the circle cannot be opened the page says so. Needs migration `0022`.

- Medicines in the calendar link. The circle's private calendar link now carries medicine times as well as appointments and reminders: one repeating entry per medicine and time of day, on the same days the Calendar page shows (every day, certain weekdays, every few days, weeks or months), from its start date and ending the day before it was stopped. Medicines taken only when needed stay out. Each entry has the medicine's name, strength and schedule, not what it is for. A switch on Add to your calendar leaves medicines out, and a new link keeps that choice. The page now says how soon Google Calendar, Apple Calendar and Outlook show a change, and that with medicines on the calendar company receives the medicine names, strengths and times and keeps its own copy; the privacy page and the health data notice say the same, with the date of the change. Semicolons in calendar text are now escaped as the standard asks. Needs migration `0023`.

- Simpler review, and nothing thrown away. Checking a visit summary is two confirms instead of one tick per section: the medicines, then everything else. Medicines marked "Please check this" sit first and must be fixed, accepted or deleted before the medicines can be confirmed; a medicine with no name cannot be accepted or saved without one. "Other notes" and "Lines we could not place" are gone: instructions that are not a medicine or a to-do go in a plain "Good to know" list. The original is shown large beside the draft, with the line an item came from marked (on a photo too, when the phone can find it); on a phone the draft and the original swap. What a visit says besides medicines, reminders and appointments is now kept with the visit: the short summary, what to watch for, good to know and questions for the next visit. It shows on Today as "From the last visit" and on a new Visits page, and people with a link see it by role: family and care managers all of it, home aides and day programs what to watch for and good to know. Today also has "Add a medication" and "Add a reminder" beside "Scan after-visit summary". Needs migration `0021`.

- Each person who helps keeps one link. After a visit or any change to medicines, the calendar, reminders or the care summary, the circle's pages show who can see that change and has not opened their link since, with "Tell Rosa" to send them the same link and a note saying what changed, or "Skip this time". Their saved link keeps working: it stops 30 days after it was last opened, and always 90 days after it was made, then "New link" sends a fresh one. A PIN is optional and off unless set. Links no longer carry the key for files. Needs migration `0019`.
- No more 12 recovery words. All Hands Care now keeps each circle's key for saved photos and PDFs and hands it to the caregiver after Google sign-in, so signing out, or a new phone or computer, never locks anyone out, and there is no "Create a key" step: a circle gets its key the first time it is needed. Photos and PDFs are still encrypted on the device before they are sent, and the stored key is wrapped with a secret kept apart from the database, so a copy of the database or of file storage alone opens nothing. All Hands Care is now technically able to open saved files. The privacy page and the health data notice lead with how they stay protected, say that plainly, and commit that we do not open them except at the caregiver's request or under a valid legal order; they no longer say "end-to-end" or "we cannot read". "Key for files" is gone from Settings. Needs migrations `0017` and `0018` and a new setting, `CIRCLE_KEY_SECRET`.
- The privacy policy has new sections: what we can and cannot read, when we share information (legal demands, safety, a change of who runs the site), where information is kept, and cookies. Vercel, Supabase and Google are named with links to their own privacy policies, and the page says what changed. How information is handled did not change.
- Nothing in the database can be run without signing in. Every database function is closed to signed-out requests, including one added with encrypted summaries that had been left open, and a test now fails if a new one is left open. Needs migration `0016`.
- Saved photos and PDFs of visit summaries are now end-to-end encrypted. Each page is encrypted on the caregiver's device before it is sent, with a key made on that device and protected by the 12 recovery words, so the server stores only bytes it cannot read. Keeping photos asks for the key for files if this device does not have it yet; opening them on a new device asks for the recovery words. Changing the key keeps every saved summary readable. Summaries saved earlier are unchanged. Needs migration `0015`.
- After a visit summary updates a circle, the last screen offers "Tell the people who help": tap a name to make that person a new link and a new 4-digit PIN, then send it by email, text or copy with the message already written. The old link and PIN stop working, and nobody's link changes unless their name is tapped.
- A shorter home page, about a sixth of the words: a navy opening with the app itself on a phone, how to add it to an iPhone or Android home screen, four short cards, three steps, a privacy band and one call to action. It no longer scrolls sideways on phones; nothing on the site can.
- The privacy page leads with four plain promises and a short note on HIPAA, followed by a full policy in sections with an effective date. It explains that a deleted circle closes at once and is erased for good after 30 days.
- Deleted circles are erased 30 days after deletion by the daily cleanup, with their people, medications and history, to-dos, events, consents and activity.
- Faster circle screens: the app now runs in Vercel's Portland region, next to the database in Oregon; each page reads its data at once instead of one query after another; the circle's sidebar and phone bar stay on screen between areas, the highlight moves as soon as you tap, and a loading view shows while a page arrives.
- New logo: two cupped navy hands holding a coral heart, with the designer's outlined wordmark. It replaces the earlier mark in the app bar, the navy sidebar (white version), the footer, share images, the favicon (a coral heart) and the iPhone and Android icons.
- Starting a circle and adding people no longer ask for 12 recovery words. Starting a circle is three steps, and a person's link always works. The key for files moved to Settings, for when a circle first keeps files; when this device has it, new links still carry it.
- The designed logo: three hands around a coral heart. The symbol is used in the app bar, the navy sidebar and share images, with the app icons for iPhone and Android and a simplified heart favicon.
- Fixes from an end-to-end test: a person is no longer added when this device cannot make their link (the key is checked first); activity and medication-history dates use the viewer's own time zone; a visit started from a circle no longer asks for names; plainer wording on the People page, the recovery words, the calendar ("You're going") and the visit summary result.
- Calendar: events with a date, a time or all day, where, notes, who is going or "needs someone to go", and repeats (every day, week, 2 weeks, month or year, until a date). An Upcoming list grouped by day and a Month grid; open to-dos show on their due date; it prints. Family can add events and say "I'll go"; others read it. Today shows what is coming up in the next two weeks and alerts when an event needs someone.
- A visit summary updates the circle. From Today or Medications, "Add a visit summary" runs the same upload and review, then shows what will change: each medication from the visit is matched to the list (add, change the strength, stop, or nothing to do), and each next step becomes a to-do with a Who picker. Nothing is saved until the caregiver confirms; the medication history and to-do notes say the change came from a visit, and Today logs it.
- To-dos: the caregiver writes what needs doing, gives it to someone, keeps it, or asks for a volunteer, with an optional date and note. Family can tap "I'll do it" from their link; anyone can check off their own. Home aides and day programs see only theirs. Views for open, mine, needs a volunteer and done. Today shows the next to-dos, a count, and who took on or finished one. On phones, areas past the fourth go under More.
- Medications: the caregiver adds each medication with its strength, how much, when (every day, some days, every few days, weeks or months, or only when needed) and what it is for. Stopping keeps it on the record with the date, and a database trigger saves every add, change, stop and restart with who made it. Helpers whose role allows it see the list, and it prints. Name suggestions come from the National Library of Medicine's public list, searched on our server. Today shows what the person takes now.
- Each circle opens on Today: four numbers (people helping, opened this week, not opened yet, allergies), recent activity with who opened their link, what needs the caregiver (locked, unopened or ending links), allergies and who to call, and the people list. Helpers see the parts their role allows. On phones the circle's areas sit in a bottom bar. The logo heart and small accents are coral.
- Each circle has exactly one caregiver who signs in: its owner. Everyone else, family included, opens their own personal link. The database allows one caregiver row per circle, and nobody can add a second one.
- Circle screens use a dark sidebar for the circle's areas on wide screens, a slim header, and smaller type (14 px on computers, 15 px on phones). Titles use DM Serif Display and text uses Geist. The installed app is named All Hands Care.
- AfterVisit is now All Hands Care, at allhandscare.org, with an interim mark until the designed logo is ready. The app installs from the browser as a phone app: a manifest, home-screen icons, and an offline page when there is no signal. Private pages are never stored on the device.
- Circle screens use a geometric sans, a soft grey page, and thin white cards. The sign-in page is a centered card. Phone headers can open How it works, Privacy, and the sample visit, and the current circle tab stays on screen. A new circle has one main heading, field errors clear as you fix them, and the people list shows a caregiver's name.
- Each circle has its own pages for Overview, Care summary, People and Settings, with tabs between them and a getting-started checklist for the owner. Starting a circle is four short steps. The home page shows the People and Care summary modules using the app's own screens with fictional data.
- Deleting a circle works, and it turns off every helper link in the same step. Caregivers can sign out from circle screens, which also removes this device's circle keys. A Google sign-in that times out shows a message on the sign-in page. The add-person picker lists only the areas a helper can see today.
- The home page describes the care circle, with "Start a care circle" and a "Sign in" link in the app bar. The sample visit still needs no sign-in. The new-circle form asks who you are caring for and no longer fills in your own name.
- Encrypt files in the browser. Each circle key is wrapped with a recovery phrase, and helper links carry the key after # so the server never sees it. Only the owner can rotate that key, and the new helper links are saved in the same step.
- A signed-in caregiver can create a circle. The circle, the owner row, consent, and the activity note are saved in one step.
- Strip share tokens, invite tokens, query strings, and hashes from Vercel Web Analytics before they are sent.
- Add circles, caregiver Google sign-in, helper links, and role permissions. Ownership stays with the owner in the database, and sign-in returns only to this site. The public demo and existing handoff links stay as they are.
- Add CI (lint, typecheck, unit tests, production build), weekly CodeQL, and Dependabot updates grouped by minor/patch and major. Runs on `main` always finish; only a newer push to the same pull request cancels a run.
- Close the critical Next.js advisories and the high Zod and PostCSS advisories (next 15.5.28, zod 4.6.7, PostCSS 8.5.29).
