# Changelog

Notable changes to All Hands Care (formerly AfterVisit). The newest entries are at the top.

## Unreleased

- To-dos: the caregiver writes what needs doing, gives it to someone, keeps it, or asks for a volunteer, with an optional date and note. Family can tap "I'll do it" from their link; anyone can check off their own. Home aides and day programs see only theirs. Views for open, mine, needs a volunteer and done. Today shows the next to-dos, a count, and who took on or finished one. On phones, areas past the fourth go under More.
- Medications: the caregiver adds each medication with its strength, how much, when (every day, some days, every few days, weeks or months, or only when needed) and what it is for. Stopping keeps it on the record with the date, and a database trigger saves every add, change, stop and restart with who made it. Helpers whose role allows it see the list, and it prints. Name suggestions come from the National Library of Medicine's public list, searched on our server. Today shows what the person takes now.
- Each circle opens on Today: four numbers (people helping, opened this week, not opened yet, allergies), recent activity with who opened their link, what needs the caregiver (locked, unopened or ending links), allergies and who to call, and the people list. Helpers see the parts their role allows. On phones the circle's areas sit in a bottom bar. The logo heart and small accents are coral.
- Each circle has exactly one caregiver who signs in: its owner. Everyone else, family included, opens their own personal link. The database allows one caregiver row per circle, and nobody can add a second one.
- Circle screens use a dark sidebar for the circle's areas on wide screens, a slim header, and smaller type (14 px on computers, 15 px on phones). Titles use DM Serif Display and text uses Geist. The installed app is named All Hands Care.
- AfterVisit is now All Hands Care, at allhandscare.org, with an interim mark until the designed logo is ready. The app installs from the browser as a phone app: a manifest, home-screen icons, and an offline page when there is no signal. Private pages are never stored on the device.
- Circle screens use a geometric sans, a soft grey page, and thin white cards. The sign-in page is a centered card. Phone headers can open How it works, Privacy, and the sample visit, and the current circle tab stays on screen. A new circle has one main heading, field errors clear as you fix them, and the people list shows a caregiver's name.
- Each circle has its own pages for Overview, Care summary, People and Settings, with tabs between them and a getting-started checklist for the owner. Starting a circle is four short steps. The home page shows the People and Care summary modules using the app's own screens with fictional data.
- Deleting a circle works, and it turns off every helper link in the same step. Caregivers can sign out from circle screens, which also removes this device's circle keys. A Google sign-in that times out shows a message on the sign-in page. The add-person picker lists only the areas a helper can see today.
- The home page describes the care circle, with "Start a care circle" and a "Sign in" link in the app bar. The sample visit still needs no sign-in. The new-circle form asks who you are caring for and no longer fills in your own name.
- Encrypt files in the browser. Each circle key is wrapped with a recovery phrase, and helper links carry the key after # so the server never sees it. Only the owner can rotate that key, and the new helper links are saved in the same step.
- A signed-in caregiver can create a circle. The circle, the owner row, consent, and the activity note are saved in one step.
- Strip share tokens, invite tokens, query strings, and hashes from Vercel Web Analytics before they are sent.
- Add circles, caregiver Google sign-in, helper links, and role permissions. Ownership stays with the owner in the database, and sign-in returns only to this site. The public demo and existing handoff links stay as they are.
- Add CI (lint, typecheck, unit tests, production build), weekly CodeQL, and Dependabot updates grouped by minor/patch and major. Runs on `main` always finish; only a newer push to the same pull request cancels a run.
- Close the critical Next.js advisories and the high Zod and PostCSS advisories (next 15.5.28, zod 4.6.7, PostCSS 8.5.29).
